# X server driver module: one entry per routine

`usr/X11R6/lib/modules/drivers/fglrx_drv.so` is the n32 shared object the XFree86 server loads for the FireGL cards. It has a dynamic symbol table and DWARF function names but no type information, so the meaning of a record field below is **inferred** from its use and the field is given as a byte offset. "Aperture" means the register window the driver keeps at driver-record +0x30 (+48); "D" is the driver record the screen record points to at screen +0xf8 (+248). Addresses are virtual addresses in the file. Static routines have no symbol; they are named by address. Callee names come from the global offset table. Labels follow `AGENTS.md` section 5: **established** means the whole routine was read.

The command-processor start-up routines of this module are in [kernel-attach.md](kernel-attach.md#command-processor-start-up-x-server-module).

## Tiling, frame offsets and mode switch

- `bR200SetTilingMode` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 1616 bytes at address 0x430dc0):
  **established.** The whole routine is read, with the two static callees (`sub_4309c0` and `sub_430940`, entries below) read as well.
  It takes the screen record in a0 (kept in s2), D from screen +0xf8 and the aperture from D +0x30, and calls `xf86GetEntityPrivate` (global slot 170, at 0x430e04) with the first word of the array at screen +0xdc and the word of `gR200EntityIndex`; the first word of the returned record is the entity record E.
  It always returns 1; there are four return instructions (0x430fe8, 0x4310d8, 0x431130, 0x431398) and none sets another value.
  The byte at D +0x5a (+90) picks the head: zero means the first head, which uses the `CRTC_OFFSET_CNTL` register at 0x228; nonzero means the second head, which uses `CRTC2_OFFSET_CNTL` at 0x328. That the nonzero case is the second head is **inferred** from those two register choices.
  Step 1, first head only (byte zero, branch at 0x430e14): sets E +0xf0 (+240) to 0 and calls the static writer `sub_430940` eight times, with surface numbers 0 to 7 in a1 and zero for a2, a3 and a4, at 0x431164, 0x431188, 0x4311ac, 0x4311d0, 0x4311f4, 0x431218, 0x431240 and 0x431264. The writer returns without a store when the aperture pointer is zero; otherwise for surface n it stores a3 at aperture +0xb04 + 16n, a4 at +0xb08 + 16n and a2 at +0xb0c + 16n, in that order. Linux `radeon_reg.h` names those `SURFACEn_LOWER_BOUND`, `SURFACEn_UPPER_BOUND` and `SURFACEn_INFO`, so this clears all eight surface windows. The second head skips this step and keeps E +0xf0 as the first head left it.
  Step 2 (both heads, at 0x430e30): clears bit 14 of the word at D +0x1fe4 (+8164).
  Step 3, tiling off (D +0x1fe0 (+8160) is zero, branch at 0x430e34): clears bits 31:30 of aperture +0x16e0 (`DEFAULT_OFFSET`, whose top two bits are the tile mode in the public `RADEON_DST_TILE_*` values) and sets bit 8 of aperture +0xb00 (`SURFACE_CNTL`; bit 8 is `RADEON_SURF_TRANSLATION_DIS`). It then clears bit 15 and bits 3:0 of aperture +0x228 for the first head (mask 0xffff7ff0, at 0x43136c), or bit 15 only of aperture +0x328 for the second head (mask 0xffff7fff, at 0x4310b8), and returns. Bit 15 is `RADEON_CRTC_TILE_EN` in the public header.
  Step 4, tiling on: clears bit 8 of aperture +0xb00 (surface translation back on) and keeps the old `DEFAULT_OFFSET` word in a stack slot (24(sp)). The word at D +0x1b68 (+7016) is compared with 2, 3 and 4. For 2, 3 and 4 it stores 1, 2 and 3 in D +0x202c (+8236) and in a stack slot (16(sp)) and sets bit 14 of D +0x1fe4; for any other value it stores 0 in both and leaves bit 14 clear.
  Step 5, buffer surfaces. Each registered buffer calls the static routine at 0x4309c0 with the buffer record address in a1, the surface number in a2 (E +0xf0 before it is incremented, the increment stored first), the lower bound in a3 and the upper bound in a4. The lower bound is the doubleword at record +0x28 (+40) minus the word at E +0x84 (+132), truncated to 32 bits. The first buffer is the record at D +0x1b60 (+7008): its upper bound is the lower bound plus (word at D +0x1b78) times the unsigned quotient of D +0x44 by (the word at screen +0xac times the word at D +0x1994), minus 0x800; a zero divisor traps (`teq`, code 7 at 0x430ee4). The later buffers use the product of two record words (+0x18 and +0x1c) minus 0x800: the record at D +0x1b90 (+7056) at 0x43135c when the word at D +0x1b2c (+6956) has bit 1 clear (reached only when D +0x19a0 (+6560) is nonzero); the record at D +0x1c80 (+7296) at 0x430f60 when D +0x23f8 (+9208) is nonzero; then, if bit 1 of D +0x1b2c is clear, the records at D +0x1cb0 (+7344) at 0x4313e0 and D +0x1bc0 (+7104) at 0x431040; with D +0x23f8 zero only the D +0x1bc0 record can follow, again under the bit-1 test. What each record is (front, back, depth) is not read; they are called buffers by their use.
  Step 6 (0x430f98): stores the old `DEFAULT_OFFSET` word with bits 31:30 replaced by the stack slot (0 to 3, shifted left 30), at aperture +0x16e0.
  Step 7, `CRTC_OFFSET_CNTL` or `CRTC2_OFFSET_CNTL` with the chip code at D +0xa0 (+160). Codes 52, 45 and 44 take one branch, every other code the other. Second head, codes 52, 45, 44: reads aperture +0x328, ANDs 0xffff5dff (clearing bits 15, 13 and 9) and ORs 0x8200 for D +0x1b68 equal to 2, 0x2200 for 3, 0xa200 for 4 (this last value is ORed into the word before the clear, so the clear does not apply), and with any other value stores the cleared word; other codes OR 0x8000 into it. First head, codes 52, 45, 44: aperture +0x228 AND 0xffff51ff then OR 0x8e00 for 2, 0x2600 for 3, 0xa600 for 4 and 0xc00 for anything else; other codes AND 0xfffffff0 then OR 0x8000.

- `sub_4309c0` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 600 bytes at address 0x4309c0, static, used by `bR200SetTilingMode`):
  **established.** It takes the screen in a0, a buffer record in a1, the surface number in a2, and lower and upper bounds in a3 and a4, and ends by jumping into the writer `sub_430940` at 0x430940 with the bounds unchanged and an info word in a2. For a surface number below 8 it first loads the current `SURFACEn_INFO` word (aperture +0xb0c + 16n, through a jump table in `.rodata`) as the starting info word; for 8 and above it starts from zero. It then clears the pitch and tile fields of that word and ORs in the new ones. For chip codes 52, 45 and 44 (checked against D +0xa0) it clears bits 12:0 and 18:16 (mask 0xfff8e000) and the pitch is screen +0xac times (screen +0x48 shifted right 3), shifted right 3 and masked to 13 bits. For other chip codes it clears bits 9:0 and 18:16 (mask 0xfff8fc00) and the pitch is the same product shifted right 4 and masked to 10 bits. The tile code, a multiple of 0x10000, comes from the word at buffer record +8: 1 gives 0, 2 gives 0x10000, 3 gives 0x20000, 4 gives 0x30000 and then, for the first set of chip codes, 7 gives 0x60000 and 8 gives 0x70000; for the other chip codes 6 gives 0x40000 and 5 gives 0x50000. Any other tile value makes it call `xf86exit` with 1 (global slot 252) and then it writes the info word with a zero tile code. The numeric tile codes have no public names that fit this chip.

- `sub_430940` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 72 bytes at address 0x430940, static):
  **established.** It takes the screen in a0, a surface number n in a1, an info word in a2 and bounds in a3 and a4. When the aperture pointer (D +0x30) is zero it returns at once; otherwise it stores a3 at aperture +0xb04 + 16n, a4 at +0xb08 + 16n and a2 at +0xb0c + 16n, in that order. It makes no call and returns nothing.

- `R200CloneAdjustFrame` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 504 bytes at address 0x422ac0):
  **established.** The whole routine is read, with its static helper at 0x422540.
  It takes a screen number in a0, an x origin in a1 and a y origin in a2, and moves where the second head scans out from. It loads the screen record from `xf86Screens` (global slot 247) indexed by a0, D from screen +0xf8, and the aperture from D +0x30.
  Origin selection by the word at D +0x1e8c (+7820): for 2 it sets y to 0 and x to the word at D +0x1eb0 (+7856), minus the word at D +0x1e94 (+7828) when that is nonzero, or to 0 when the word at D +0x1e90 (+7824) is nonzero. For 3 it sets x to 0 and y to the word at D +0x1ec4 (+7876), minus D +0x1e94 when that is nonzero, or 0 when D +0x1e90 is nonzero. For any other value x and y stay as passed.
  Tiling off (D +0x1fe0 zero): calls the helper with x and y, then stores its result at aperture +0x324 (`CRTC2_OFFSET`) and returns.
  Tiling on, chip code at D +0xa0 equal to 52, 45 or 44: calls the helper with x and y both 0, stores the result at aperture +0x324, ORs 0x200 into aperture +0x328, then reads aperture +0x358 and discards the value (a load into the zero register at 0x422c0c) and stores x ORed with y shifted left 16 there, which the public header names `R300_CRTC2_TILE_X0_Y0`.
  Tiling on, any other chip code: calls the helper with x and y, stores into the low four bits of aperture +0x328 the low four bits of y (computed as the absolute value masked with 0xf, negated back for a negative y and masked again, which equals y and 0xf), keeps the other bits, and then stores the helper's result at aperture +0x324. The 0x328 store comes first.
  The routine has no return value; it leaves the aperture pointer in v0 (the `ld` from 16(sp) before each of its three returns).
  The helper at 0x422540 writes through its a3 pointer the word at screen +0x330 plus a byte offset. With bytes per pixel b = screen +0x48 shifted right 3 (signed) and pitch p = the word at screen +0xac: untiled (D +0x1fe0 zero) the offset is b times (x plus p times y); tiled it is 8 times (b times p times (y shifted right 3) plus (x divided by (0x100 divided by b)) times 0x100), plus (y and 7) times 0x100, plus b times the remainder of that division of x. When the word at screen +0x48 is 24 the offset is first divided by 24 and multiplied by 24 again.

- `R200EnterVT` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 636 bytes at address 0x422cc0):
  **established.** The whole routine is read; its callees are named by verified target and their bodies are not read here. It brings one screen back after a virtual-terminal switch: it re-runs the mode and frame set-up, restarts the command processor and turns the overlay paths back on. It takes the screen number in a0 (kept in s2), loads the screen record from `xf86Screens` (global slot 247), D from screen +0xf8 and the aperture from D +0x30; the aperture is kept at 0(sp).
  It returns 1 at 0x422e40 on success and 0 at 0x422f20 when `sub_422040` returns zero.
  Order of events:
  1. For chip codes 47 and 49 (the word at D +0xa0, branches at 0x422d08 and 0x422d14) it clears bits 25:24 of aperture +0x28 (`BIOS_6_SCRATCH` in the public header; mask 0xfcffffff, store at 0x422ed8).
  2. It calls the static routine at 0x422040 (through `bgezall zero`, an always-taken call at 0x422d28) with the screen and the word at screen +0xcc (+204). A zero return ends the routine with 0. The decompiled outline of that routine (its callees are not read here) is: it returns 0 when a static routine at 0x421600 returns zero or when `firegl_SyncDacs` returns nonzero (that call is made only when the word at D +0x1e8c is above 1); otherwise it stores 1 at screen +0x368, stores its second argument at D +0x1998 and returns 1.
  3. With the byte at D +0x5a (+90) zero and the word at D +0x18f8 (+6392) nonzero it calls `R200EngineRestore` (global slot 449, at 0x422ee8).
  4. It picks an x and a y. With the byte at D +0x5d (+93) zero, or the word at D +0x1e8c below 2, or the word at D +0x1e90 not equal to 1, they are the words at screen +176 and +180. Otherwise it stores 0 to both of those words and uses 0 and 0. It calls the function pointer in the word at screen +0x40c (+1036; this is **inferred** to be the screen's frame-adjust hook) with the screen number, x, y and 0 (jump at 0x422d88).
  5. If the word at D +0x6c (+108) is nonzero it calls `R200CloneAdjustFrame` (global slot 471, at 0x422dd0) with the screen number, x and y and 0. With the word at D +0x1e8c below 2, x and y are the words at D +0x70 and +0x74; otherwise those two words are first set to 0 (stores at 0x422db0 and 0x422db8) and 0 and 0 are passed.
  6. If the word at D +0x19a4 (+6564) is nonzero and the byte at D +0x5a is zero, then when the word at D +0x18f8 is nonzero it calls `xf86EnablePciBusMaster` (global slot 329) with the word at D +4 and 1, then `FGLStartCP` (slot 487, whose entry is in [kernel-attach.md](kernel-attach.md#command-processor-start-up-x-server-module)) with D and 1; either way it then calls `DRIUnlock` (slot 158) with the word at screen +8.
  7. With the byte at D +0x5a zero, the word at screen +0x10c (+268) nonzero and the word at screen +0x48 equal to 32 it calls `FIREGL_EnableOverlay` (slot 514) with the screen and 1.
  8. If the word at D +0x23f8 (+9208) is nonzero it calls `QBS_EnableOverlay` (slot 516) and `QBS_EnableStereo` (slot 519), each with the screen and 1, and then, when the word at D +0x2420 (+9248) is nonzero, stores it at aperture +0x228 (`CRTC_OFFSET_CNTL`, store at 0x422e24). The matching save of that register is in `R200LeaveVT`.

- `R200LeaveVT` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 284 bytes at address 0x422f40):
  **established.** The whole routine is read; its callees are named by verified target and their bodies are not read here. It undoes the work of `R200EnterVT` when the server gives up the console: it saves the display offset control register, switches the overlay paths off, takes the DRI lock and stops the command processor. It takes the screen number in a0, loads the screen record from `xf86Screens` and D from screen +0xf8, and returns nothing; the register v0 holds whatever the last callee left.
  1. If the word at D +0x23f8 (+9208) is nonzero it copies the word at aperture +0x228 (`CRTC_OFFSET_CNTL`, aperture from D +0x30) to D +0x2420 (+9248), at 0x422f88.
  2. With the byte at D +0x5a zero, the word at screen +0x10c nonzero and the word at screen +0x48 equal to 32 (0x423000 to 0x423010) it calls `FIREGL_EnableOverlay` (global slot 514) with the screen and 0 and reloads D +0x23f8.
  3. If D +0x23f8 is nonzero it calls `QBS_EnableOverlay` (slot 516) and `QBS_EnableStereo` (slot 519), each with the screen and 0.
  4. If the word at D +0x19a4 (+6564) is nonzero and the byte at D +0x5a is zero it calls `DRILock` (slot 159) with the word at screen +8 and 10, and then, if the word at D +0x18f8 (+6392) is nonzero, `FGLStartCP` (slot 487) with D and 0.
  5. It ends by calling the static routine at 0x41dcc0 with the screen (an always-taken `bgezall zero` call at 0x422fe0) and returning.

## Gamma and palette

- `FGLSetGammaData` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 1700 bytes at address 0x42fe00):
  **established.** The whole routine is read. It rebuilds the 10-bit palette of one head from a packed gamma setting, writing the palette through the index and data registers one entry at a time.
  It takes the screen record in a0 and a head number in a1, D from screen +0xf8 and the aperture from D +0x30. It returns nothing.
  Head selection at 0x42fe4c: a1 equal to 0 clears bit 5 of aperture +0x7c (`DAC_CNTL2`, where the public header names bit 5 `RADEON_DAC2_PALETTE_ACC_CTL`) and reads the packed word at D +0x1fd4 (+8148); a1 equal to 1 sets that bit and reads D +0x1fd8 (+8152); any other a1 leaves the bit alone and reads D +0x1fd8.
  Each packed word holds three 10-bit fields, bits 29:20, 19:10 and 9:0. Each is divided by 100.0 as a float and passed to `sub_42fd80`, in the order bits 29:20, then 19:10, then 9:0 (calls at 0x42fec0, 0x42fed0, 0x42fee0). The three results are fixed-point gamma values with 16 fractional bits; the first is kept at 32(sp), the second in s3 and the third in v0.
  It then reads aperture +0x58 (`DAC_CNTL`), sets bit 8 (`RADEON_DAC_8BIT_EN`), clears bit 14 and stores it back at 0x42fef8. This value, in a5, is kept for one path below.
  The word at screen +0x48 (bits per pixel) selects the loop: a value of 8, or any value other than 16 and 32, skips the palette write.
  Common loop for 32 bits per pixel (0x42ff6c) and for 16 bits per pixel when D +0x23f8 (+9208) is nonzero (0x4300e4, joined at 0x430104): it divides 1.0 by each fixed-point gamma converted back to a double (the low 16 bits times 2^-16 plus the high half), stores zero in the byte at aperture +0xb3 once, and runs 256 passes. Pass i takes x = i times 2^-8 and calls `xf86pow` three times, with the reciprocals of the 32(sp), s3 and v0 values in that order. Each result is multiplied by 1023.0, 0.5 is added and the sum is truncated to a word. The result for the 32(sp) value goes to bits 29:20, the s3 value to bits 19:10 and the v0 value to bits 9:0 of one word, stored to aperture +0xb8 (`PALETTE_30_DATA` in the public header) at 0x430250 or 0x4300c0 (the two copies of the loop). After each store it calls `FGLMDelay` (global slot 479) with 50.
  Loop for 16 bits per pixel when D +0x23f8 is zero (0x4302d8): the same arithmetic over 64 passes with x = i times 2^-6, and the byte at aperture +0xb3 receives i shifted left 2 (0x4303ac) before each store to aperture +0xb8 (0x430434). After the loop it stores the saved `DAC_CNTL` value from a5 to aperture +0xb8 (0x430448), ORs 0xff into aperture +0xb0 (0x43044c to 0x430454) and finishes. That the last store goes to the palette data register and not to `DAC_CNTL` is read from the instruction at 0x430448 (offset 184), not interpreted.
  Every path that reaches the end clears bit 5 of aperture +0x7c again (the 8-bit path at 0x42ff4c, the others at 0x430458 and after the 256-pass loops through 0x42ff24). One store to the palette data register is not a table entry: the `DAC_CNTL` value kept in a5, stored at 0x430448 after the 64-pass loop. Whether that store is intended is not decided here. Which of the three fields is red, green or blue is **inferred** only from the order of the 10-bit fields in the public palette register.

- `Rage6SetGamma16Correction` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 548 bytes at address 0x42e340):
  **established.** The whole routine is read. It loads a 256-entry table of 16-bit colour values into the card's 10-bit palette of one head, using the same palette registers as `FGLSetGammaData` but with no floating-point step. It takes the screen record in a0, a head number in a1 and in a2 a pointer to the table, takes D from screen +0xf8 and the aperture from D +0x30, and returns nothing.
  Head selection runs only when bit 4 of the word at D +0x1e74 (+7796) is set (test at 0x42e360): a1 equal to 0 clears bit 5 of aperture +0x7c (`DAC_CNTL2`, mask 0xffffffdf, store at 0x42e4c4), a1 equal to 1 sets it (store at 0x42e4d4), any other a1 leaves it. The same bit-4 test guards the closing clear of bit 5 at 0x42e478.
  It then sets bit 8 and clears bit 14 of aperture +0x58 (`DAC_CNTL`, store at 0x42e3b0), as `FGLSetGammaData` does.
  The word at screen +0x48 (bits per pixel) selects the load: 8 and any value other than 16 and 32 load nothing.
  32 bits per pixel (0x42e3d8): stores 0 in the byte at aperture +0xb3, then makes 128 passes over the table, 16 bytes a pass, each pass storing two words to aperture +0xb8 (`PALETTE_30_DATA`): the first from the 16-bit values at table +0, +2 and +4 of the pass, the second from those at +8, +10 and +12. Each value is shifted right 6 to 10 bits; the value at +0 goes to bits 29:20, the one at +2 to bits 19:10 and the one at +4 to bits 9:0. The halfwords at +6 and +14 are not read. That is 256 entries of 8 bytes. There is no delay between stores.
  16 bits per pixel (0x42e4d8): 64 passes with i from 0 to 63. Before each store it writes the byte i shifted left 2 to aperture +0xb3. The word stored to aperture +0xb8 takes bits 29:20 from halfword +0 and bits 9:0 from halfword +4 of table entry ((i >> 3) or ((i >> 1) << 3)), and bits 19:10 from halfword +2 of table entry ((i << 2) or (i >> 4)), each entry being 8 bytes and each value shifted right 6. After each store it calls `FGLMDelay` (global slot 479) with 50. After the loop it ORs 0xff into aperture +0xb0 (`PALETTE_INDEX`, at 0x42e558).
  Which colour each field carries is **inferred** only from the order of the three fields in the public 10-bit palette word.

- `sub_42fd80` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 88 bytes at address 0x42fd80, static, used by `FGLSetGammaData`):
  **established.** It takes a float in f12 and returns in v0 the float times 65536.0 truncated to an integer (a 16.16 fixed-point number), except that a value below 0.1 (compared as a double at 0x42fd8c, against the double constant at 0x4680f0) or above 10.0 (compared as a float at 0x42fda4, against the float at 0x46807c) returns 65536, which is 1.0. A NaN fails both tests and takes the multiply. It makes no call and touches no register of the card.

## Clock PLL access

- `ulRC6PllReadUlong` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 44 bytes at address 0x42e940):
  **established.** The whole routine is read. It reads one PLL register through the index and data pair of the register window. It takes the aperture base in a0 and the PLL register number in a1, and returns the data word. It makes no call.
  It loads the word at aperture +8 (`CLOCK_CNTL_INDEX`), replaces its low six bits with a1 masked to six bits and clears bit 7 (the write-enable bit `RADEON_PLL_WR_EN` in the public header), and stores the result at aperture +8 (0x42e95c). It then reads the word at aperture +12 (`CLOCK_CNTL_DATA`, 0x42e960) as the return value, and stores the original word of +8 back (0x42e968, in the delay slot of the return), so the index register is left as it was found.

- `vRC6PllWriteUlong` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 76 bytes at address 0x42e980):
  **established.** The whole routine is read. It changes one PLL register through the same pair. It takes the aperture base in a0, the PLL register number in a1, the bits to set in a2 and a mask of bits to keep in a3. It returns nothing and makes no call.
  Stores in order: (1) the word at aperture +8 with its low six bits replaced by a1 and bit 7 cleared (0x42e99c); (2) it reads aperture +12 (0x42e9a0); (3) the same index word with bit 7 set, enabling the write (0x42e9a8); (4) a2 ORed with (the word just read ANDed with a3) to aperture +12 (0x42e9b4); (5) the word at aperture +8 read back with its low six bits and bit 7 cleared (mask 0xffffff40, stored at 0x42e9c8). The index is therefore left as 0 in its low six bits, not restored to its value on entry, unlike `ulRC6PllReadUlong`.

## Frame offset registers

- `vCalcCRTCOffsetRegs` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 328 bytes at address 0x430c40):
  **established.** The whole routine is read. It works out the scan-out start address of one head from that head's x and y, and returns the head's offset-control word with its tile-line bits cleared. It takes the screen record in a0, a base address in a1, an output pointer for the address in a2, an output pointer for the control word in a3 and a head number in a4. It takes D from screen +0xf8 and the aperture from D +0x30, and returns nothing.
  Head record: the x is the word at D + 16 times head + 0x200c (+8204) and the y the word at D + 16 times head + 0x2010 (+8208).
  Control word: for head 0 it reads aperture +0x228 (`CRTC_OFFSET_CNTL`, load at 0x430d24), otherwise aperture +0x328 (`CRTC2_OFFSET_CNTL`, load at 0x430c60). It stores the word through a3 and then stores it again through a3 with the low four bits cleared (mask -16, stores at 0x430c64 and 0x430c6c); the second store is what remains. It writes no register.
  Address: with bytes per pixel b = the word at screen +0x48 shifted right 3 (signed) and pitch p = the word at screen +0xac, bit 14 of the word at D +0x1fe4 (+8164) clear gives b times (x plus p times y). With that bit set (the bit `bR200SetTilingMode` sets when tiling is on) the address is 8 times (b times p times (y shifted right 3) plus (x divided by (0x100 divided by b)) times 0x100), plus (y and 7) times 0x100, plus b times the remainder of that division of x. A zero b or a zero quotient traps (`teq`, code 7 at 0x430ce4 and 0x430d0c). When the word at screen +0x48 is 24 the address is divided by 24 and multiplied by 24 again. The result plus a1 is stored through a2 (0x430d20 or 0x430d84). The same arithmetic is in the static helper that `R200CloneAdjustFrame` uses, except that helper adds a screen word at +0x330 and not an argument.

## Image sync and swap barrier

- `SGIGLXSwapBarrierInit` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 492 bytes at address 0x430740):
  **established.** The whole routine is read. It turns on the swap-barrier extension for one screen when the image-sync option is present, and switches on the pad lines the image-sync path needs. It takes the screen record in a0, D from screen +0xf8, the screen number from screen +0xc and the aperture from D +0x30, and returns nothing.
  It reads a pointer P, the word at D +0x247c (+9340), twice. If bit 1 of the word at P +0x50 is set (0x430764), it sets bit 11 in D +0x1b28 (+6952) and calls `xf86DrvMsg` with type 7 and the text "SGIVC: swap buffers on vertical blank enabled."
  It keeps a global table of 8-byte records, with the pointer at 0x4681a8 and a capacity count at 0x4681ac. When the capacity is not above the screen number it adds 16 to the capacity (0x4308cc) and calls `Xrealloc` with the old pointer and the new capacity times 8 (0x4308d0); the new pointer is stored at 0x4681a8. It then calls `xf86bzero` on the record at index screen number times 8, with length 8 (0x4307cc).
  If the first word at P is zero (branch at 0x4307dc), it calls `xf86DrvMsg` with type 7 and the text "SGIX_swap_barrier disabled because ImageSync functionality unavailable." and returns (0x430924 to 0x4308b8).
  Otherwise it stores 1 in the second word of the screen's record (0x4307fc), calls `xf86DrvMsg` with "SGIX_swap_barrier enabled." and calls `__glXSwapBarrierInit` with the screen number and the address of a two-word block on the stack. The words are the static routine addresses 0x430600 and 0x430700, stored at sp+0 and sp+4 (a callback pair; what the two routines do is not read here).
  Chip code 44 (the word at D +0xa0, test at 0x43083c): calls `xf86DrvMsg` with "SGIX_swap_barrier disabled second port DVI output in R300 family." and clears bit 25 of aperture +0x288 (`FP2_GEN_CNTL`; mask 0xfdffffff at 0x4308f8 to 0x430900).
  All enabled paths then clear bit 0 of aperture +0x1a0 (`GPIOPAD_EN`, mask 0xfffffffe, at 0x430854), set bit 0 of aperture +0x198 (`GPIOPAD_MASK`, at 0x430864), write the low byte of aperture +0x8 (`CLOCK_CNTL_INDEX`) as 0x8d, keeping the upper bits (0x43087c to 0x430880), and OR 0x800000 into aperture +0xc (`CLOCK_CNTL_DATA`, at 0x430890 to 0x430894). Index 0x8d is PLL register 0x0d with the write-enable bit 7 (`RADEON_PLL_WR_EN`) set; the write-enable stays set when the routine returns. Which PLL register 0x0d is for this chip is **inferred**: it is `SCLK_CNTL` in the public headers for the Radeon family, and bit 23 is not named there.

## Command processor initialisation

- `FGLInitCP` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 720 bytes at address 0x42c4c0):
  **established.** The whole routine is read, with its two static table fillers. It checks the ring size, programs the ring location and the two write-back addresses, and passes a 32-byte description of the ring to the kernel module's `firegl_InitCP`. It takes the screen record in a0 and a pointer in a1 to a caller buffer, takes D from screen +0xf8 and the aperture from D +0x30, and returns 1 on success and 0 on any failure.
  Set-up: reads the word at D +0x19ac (+6572) into a stack slot (32(sp)), calls `xf86GetEntityPrivate` (as in `bR200SetTilingMode`; the first word of the returned record is the entity record E) and clears 3704 bytes (0xe78) of the caller's buffer with `xf86memset` (0x42c528). It stores 0 at buffer +0x208 (+520).
  Ring size: the word at E +0x40 is a byte count (**inferred** from the shift to dwords and the dword wording of the message). Buffer +0x210 (+528) receives that count shifted right 2, in 32-bit words. If that is below 0xd000 (the unsigned compare at 0x42c540) the routine logs "[CP] ring buffer too small! (dRingSize=%ld dwords, minimum=%ld)" through `xf86DrvMsg` with type 5, and returns 0. Otherwise buffer +0x214 (+532) receives the byte count shifted right 4 minus 1, capped at 0x3fff, and buffer +0x218 (+536) receives 0x1f.
  Chip code, the word at D +0xa0: for 8, 47 and 49 it runs the static filler at 0x42be40 on the address buffer +0x208; for 44, 45 and 52 it runs the static filler at 0x42bec0 on the same address; for any other code it logs "[CP] unsupported ASIC!" with type 5 and returns 0 (0x42c5c8 to 0x42c5e4).
  Then, for chip codes 8, 47 and 49 only, it ORs 0x110 into aperture +0x26ac and issues a `sync` (0x42c644 to 0x42c64c). For every accepted chip code it stores 3 at aperture +0x1724 (`CACHE_CNTL`, store at 0x42c664) and issues a `sync`.
  It then calls, each with the aperture, E and the caller's buffer: `r200_RegisterRingBufferLocation` (address 0x42bf80), `r200_EnableTimeStampWriteBack` (address 0x42c200) and `r200_EnableRPTRwriteback` (address 0x42c1c0), in that order. Their bodies are read in [the start-up entries](kernel-attach.md#command-processor-start-up-x-server-module). Next it stores 0 at aperture +0x2140 (store at 0x42c6b0, then `sync`).
  Kernel hand-off: clears a 32-byte block on the stack with `xf86memset` and fills it in this order: the doubleword at E +0x18 at sp+0, the doubleword at E +0x38 at sp+8, the doubleword at E +0x58 at sp+16, the word at E +0x50 at sp+24 and the word at E +0x54 at sp+28. It calls `firegl_InitCP` (global slot 377, at 0x42c6f0) with the stack slot saved from D +0x19ac and the address of that block. A zero result returns 1. A nonzero result is passed to `GetErrorName`; the routine then logs "[CP] firegl_InitCP failed! (%d=%s)" with type 5 and returns 0.
  What each E field is (ring base, size, write-back address) is not read here; they are copied unchanged.

- `sub_42be40` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 92 bytes at address 0x42be40, static):
  **established.** It takes a pointer in a0 and stores twelve constants relative to it, in this order: +0x6c = 0xc97, +0x4c = 0x76000000, +0x68 = 3, +0x64 = 1, +0x60 = 0, +0x58 = 0x7ff, +0x5c = 0x10, +0x54 = 0x736, +0x50 = 0, +0x48 = 0x70000000, +0x44 = 0x10000000 and +0x40 = 0x9bc. It reads nothing and makes no call. Its caller passes buffer +0x208, so these land at +0x248 to +0x274 of the buffer. `FGLInitCP` runs it for chip codes 8, 47 and 49.

- `sub_42bec0` (`fglrx_drv.so`, 96 bytes at address 0x42bec0, static):
  **established.** The same shape as `sub_42be40` with different values, stored in this order: +0x6c = 0x1393, +0x4c = 2, +0x48 = 0xfffe, +0x44 = 0xfffe, +0x50 = 0xffff, +0x68 = 4, +0x64 = 4, +0x58 = 0x1fff, +0x5c = 0xd, +0x54 = 0x10ec, +0x40 = 0x10f4 and +0x60 = 0xb405a0. `FGLInitCP` runs it for chip codes 44, 45 and 52. The two fillers store different values at the same offsets (for example +0x50 is 0 in one and 0xffff in the other), so the two groups of chip codes get different tables; the numbers have no public names that fit.
