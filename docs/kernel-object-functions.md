# Kernel object: open, release, detach and board-identity routines

`usr/cpu/sysgen/IP35boot/fglrx.o` is a relocatable 64-bit MIPS object with a symbol table and DWARF function names. Calls to other objects are relocations, so a callee is named by the relocation; a call to a routine inside the object is named by its symbol, or by its address as `sub_ADDR` when it has no symbol. Several small routines (at 0xed68, 0xed94, 0x13eac, 0x16168, 0x1613c and 0x1b98c among them) do nothing but spill their argument registers to the stack and return: they are disabled debug-print stubs, and a call to one of them has no effect. The attach path is in [kernel-attach.md](kernel-attach.md); register accesses are in [registers.md](registers.md).

Records. The board record is reached from `hwgraph_fastinfo_get`; its word at +428 is the board number, the doubleword at +696 the interrupt handle, +560 and +568 a buffer and its size, +544 a PIO map, and +412 the graph vertex. The tables `[.data+312]` (4-byte entries) and `[.data+328]` (8-byte entries) are indexed by board number. `[.data+320]` is a word that `fglrx_init` zeroes; `fglrx_open` uses it as the bound for the board number and as the first slot of the per-open table (`.bss+16`, 8-byte entries, 65536 slots). The per-open record is 56 bytes and the device-private structure is 11680 bytes.

## Opening and releasing the device

- `fglrx_open` (`fglrx.o`, 1268 bytes at address 0x2950): **Inferred.**
  Arguments: a0 points at a device number word and a1 holds the open flags. Returns 0, or the result of `firegl_open`, or a positive error number.
  Checks in order. Flag bit 0x400 set: logs through `cmn_err` and returns -16. The board field (bits 26 to 18 of the number word, mask 0x1ff) is zero: calls `hwgraph_fastinfo_get`, logs the board number (+428) and returns 19. The low 18 bits (the board number) are not below `[.data+320]`: logs and returns 19. The `[.data+312]` entry for that board is zero: logs and returns 19.
  Then it takes a mutex (`mutex_lock`, priority 25), scans upward from slot `[.data+320]`, to a limit of 65536, for the first zero entry of the per-open table, and allocates 56 bytes with `kmem_zalloc` (call at 0x2ab8). A full table releases the mutex, logs and returns 16; a failed allocation releases the mutex, logs and returns 12.
  On success it stores the new record in the slot, stores the board record in the record's first doubleword, rewrites the number word with the slot index in the low 18 bits and the board field above it, stores the open flags at record +16, calls `firegl_open` (call at 0x2b70) and, in that call's delay slot, clears the word at record +48. It sets that word to 1 when `firegl_open` returned 0, then bumps the atomic counter at `[.data+272]` with a load-linked loop (0x2b84 to 0x2b94). When the counter at `[.data+288]` is still zero it increments it with a second loop (0x2bc4 to 0x2bd4), saves the global `pcibr_pioerr_dump` at +8 of the open table and clears the global. The routine returns the value `firegl_open` left in v0.
- `firegl_open` (`fglrx.o`, 940 bytes at address 0xf114): **Inferred.**
  Takes the file in a1. It calls `__ke_get_file_bdata` (call at 0xf150), reads the device-private pointer from +208 of what that returns, and the doubleword at +8 of that structure; a zero there logs and returns -19 (0xf260).
  Otherwise it logs the open count at +68 and calls `firegl_open_helper` (0xf1c8) with the file and the structure. A nonzero result is logged and returned. On a zero result it calls `__ke_get_file_priv` (0xf27c), creates a mutex with `firegl_new_mutex` and stores it at +28 of the file's private record, registers `fglrx_add_thread_exit_callback` (0xf2d8), calls `__ke_mod_inc_use_count` (0xf300), bumps the atomic at +80 and takes the spin lock to raise the open count at +68.
  Only when that count becomes 1 does it call `sub_10378` (0xf3a4, which sets two atomics and logs). A nonzero result of that call is kept as the return value and the count is lowered again; when the count is then 0, it reads the atomic at +72 and the word at +64: if both are zero it calls `firegl_takedown` (0xf4b0), otherwise it logs and returns -16 (0xf498).
  Returns: -19, the helper's error, the first-open call's error, -16, or 0 on success.
- `firegl_release_helper` (`fglrx.o`, 2736 bytes at address 0x143b0): **Inferred.**
  Takes the file in a1 and returns 0 on its single exit (0x14908). The per-file record is the result of `__ke_get_file_priv` (call at 0x143f8, kept in s1); the device-private structure is its doubleword at +72 (s0); the process id comes from `__ke_getpid` (s6). The calls and the conditions that guard them were read; the bodies of the callees, including the lock protocol, were not.
  Setup (0x14490 to 0x144e4): logs, calls `__ke_get_file_bdata`, and when the per-file word at +300 is nonzero calls `is1_unbind` and clears the word.
  Lock handling: when the device doubleword at +104 (the lock word pointer) is nonzero the code at 0x14910 runs. It tests bit 31 of the lock word and compares the device doubleword at +120 with the per-file record: on a match it calls `_r6x_resetPM4` (0x14994) and `firegl_lock_free` (0x14a04, 0x14adc), under `__ke_spin_lock` and `__ke_spin_unlock`, and `firegl_checklock_drm_buffers` (0x14a54) and `firegl_unlock_drm_buffers` (0x14b30) around it; otherwise it calls `firegl_lock_device` in loops (fourth argument 0 at 0x14b54 and 0x14b78, 2 at 0x14c60 and 0x14c84) until it returns zero, then frees the lock with `firegl_lock_free` (0x14db0 and 0x14e04). `drm_remove_magic` (0x14d80) runs with the per-file word at +32. `kthread_get_pid` (0x14e50) supplies a pid for one log line.
  Teardown (0x14504 to 0x148f8): the per-file record is unlinked from the device's doubly linked list (pointers at +56 and +64, device list words at +520 and +528) between `mrupdatef` (0x14564) and `mrunlock` (0x145a4); `__ke_down` and `__ke_up` on the semaphore at +96 bracket the stereo step, which calls `firegl_enable_stereo_on_device(device, 0)` (0x14684) when the per-file word at +296 is nonzero and clears it; the per-file list at +208 moves onto the device's free list at +1144 under `__ke_down_struct_sem`, raising the counters at +1152 and +1176; each element of the list at +264 goes to `firegl_remove_mutex` (0x14754); each element of the list at +240 goes to `drm_mem_dec_locked_pages` and then `drm_free` (0x147d8, 0x147ec); `firegl_texture_died` is called with the per-file id at +28 (0x14830); `freesema` releases the semaphore at +96 (0x14850) and `drm_free` frees it, using `__ke_sema_size` for its size (0x1487c, 0x14890); the 304-byte record is cleared to 0xff with `__ke_memset` (0x148b8) and freed with `drm_free` (0x148cc).

## Detach, initialisation and device identity

- `fglrx_detach` (`fglrx.o`, 1072 bytes at address 0x1ef4): **Inferred.**
  Takes the PCI connection point in a0. It reads configuration space from offset 9 (three bytes, `pciio_config_get`) (call at 0x1f24) and returns 5 unless the class is 3 and the subclass 0 (a display controller, VGA-compatible). A failed `hwgraph_traverse` (call at 0x1f9c) logs through `cmn_err` and returns 6.
  Otherwise it takes the board record from `hwgraph_fastinfo_get`, and raises `assfail` if the `[.data+328]` entry for the board number at +428 is not that record. Then: `firegl_cleanup_module`; when +696 holds an interrupt handle, `pciio_intr_disconnect`, `pciio_intr_free`, and a zero store; `bzero` of a 1024-byte stack buffer and a `sprintf`; `hwgraph_edge_remove`, `hwgraph_inventory_remove` (class 7, type 24 as the constants in a1 and a2) and a second `hwgraph_edge_remove`; `hwgraph_vertex_destroy`; the vertex word at +412 is set to -1; the buffer at +560 is freed with `kmem_free` and zeroed; the PIO map at +544 is finished with `pciio_piomap_done` and `pciio_piomap_free` and zeroed.
  The last steps take `GfxBoardInitSema` with `psema` (priority 25), call `sub_2760` (a board-pointer check against `GfxBoardLimit`), zero the board's entries in `[.data+328]` and `[.data+312]`, release with `vsema`, and return 0.
- `fglrx_init` (`fglrx.o`, 1172 bytes at address 0x84): **Established.**
  Takes no arguments and returns what `pciio_iterate` returns.
  It allocates two zeroed arrays with `kmem_zalloc` (calls at 0xe8 and 0x114): `GfxBoardLimit` 8-byte entries stored at `[.data+280]` and `GfxBoardLimit` 4-byte entries stored at `[.data+336]`, then calls `sub_2324` (a check of the board-pointer tables, call at 0x14c). For each board number below `GfxBoardLimit`, it calls `find_fglrx_pipe(number, &halfword, &doubleword)` (call at 0x1e0) and, when the result is positive: `sub_25cc` with the number and the halfword (call at 0x1f0), `test_and_set_int` on the 4-byte entry of `[.data+312]` (0x21c), stores the halfword at board record +480 (0x240), calls `GfxRegisterBoard(.data, record, record + 224)` (0x264) and stores the doubleword from `find_fglrx_pipe` at +528 in its delay slot, then `spinlock_init` on the matching `[.data+336]` entry with its delay slot clearing the `[.data+280]` entry (0x280).
  After the loop it clears 524288 bytes at `.bss+16` with `bzero` (0x300), calls `init_mutex` on `.bss+0x80000+16` (0x344), initialises two multiple-reader locks, `fglrx_irixMallocLock` and `fglrx_irixMapLock` (0x388 and 0x3b0) with a zero store at `[.data+320]`, clears `drm_mem_allocated` with a load-linked and store-conditional loop (0x3e8 to 0x3f4), calls `fglrx_irix_avl_init` (0x3fc) and `kmem_zone_init(32, ...)` (0x420) whose result goes to `fglrx_QCL_zone`. It then sets the byte at +82 to 1 in each of `rrm_maxrn` 176-byte `RRMrn` entries (0x4a4) and ends with `pciio_iterate` and the prefix `fglrx_` (0x4e0), the call that replays found devices to `fglrx_attach`.
- `fglrxReadCompositorNic` (`fglrx.o`, 1764 bytes at address 0x8830): **Inferred.**
  Takes the compositor handle in a0 and an output record in a1; returns 0 on success and -1 on failure. It reads a block of bytes from the compositor board's serial chip through `sub_5614` (ten call sites, at 0x889c, 0x88c4, 0x88f0, 0x8960, 0x899c, 0x8a24, 0x8a74, 0x8b04, 0x8b4c and 0x8bcc). That helper takes the handle, a command byte, an index and an output address, uses bits 15 to 8 of the handle's word at +328 to pick the device, and passes the request to `usb_comp_readwrite_register`; every call here passes the command byte 165 (0xa5).
  The record gets 255 in bytes +10 and +11 and 1 in byte +12 first. The first read, at index 3, gives a value that the routine multiplies by 8 and adds 6 to (kept to eight bits); it reads a length byte at that index and moves on by the length (low five bits) plus 1 without reading that field's data. Then four fields follow, each a length byte (low five bits kept) at one index and that many data bytes at the next indexes.
  The four fields are stored right-aligned in fixed widths and padded on the left with spaces (32) when the length is below the width: 14 bytes at +60, 10 bytes at +13, 19 bytes at +23 and 6 bytes at +44. The first six data bytes of the second field are also stored at +4 to +9, and in the third field every byte equal to 0x5f ('_') is stored as 45 ('-').
  After the fields it stores the ASCII digit 48 ('0') at +50, +51, +52, +53, +55, +56, +57 and +58 and 255 at +54 and +59, and returns 0.
  Each of the ten reads that returns a negative value goes to its own error block: the block calls `printf` with a message, stores 1 in the first word of the record and returns -1.
  What the fields hold is an open sub-fact: unknown. The layout (offset in 8-byte units, then length-prefixed fields) is read from the code; no public format was matched to it.
- `_r6x_IsDeviceSupported` (`fglrx.o`, 5752 bytes at address 0x11f84): **Inferred.**
  Takes the device-private structure in a0 and returns 1 if one of the listed PCI devices is present, otherwise 0 (the return is `record +28 != 0`, set at 0x120c8 and 0x120dc).
  It clears 16 bytes at +16 with `__ke_memset`, then looks for vendor 0x1002 devices one after another with `__ke_pci_find_device(0x1002, id, [a0+11656] + 440)` (51 calls, the first at 0x11fe4), storing a match at +8 and stopping the chain at the first match. The ids in order are 0x5148, 0x514c, 0x514d, 0x516c, 0x4242, 0x4147, 0x4144, 0x4145, 0x4146, 0x4e47, 0x4e44, 0x4154, 0x4155, 0x4156, 0x4157, 0x4150, 0x4152, 0x4151, 0x4e45, 0x4e46, 0x4144, 0x4145, 0x4146, 0x4148, 0x4e4b, 0x4e48, 0x4e49, 0x4e4a, 0x4e50, 0x4e54, 0x4964 to 0x4967, 0x496c to 0x496f, 0x4c64 to 0x4c67, 0x4c6c to 0x4c6f, 0x5961, 0x5960, 0x5c61, 0x5c63 and 0x5964.
  A match reads four configuration words through `__ke_pci_read_config_word` (offsets 0, 2, 0x2c and 0x2e) into +20, +22, +24 and +26 and sets three record fields: the identifier word at +28, a small class word at +32 (values 1 to 4) and flag bits ORed into +16 (7, 0xf, plus 0x20 for one subsystem). The identifier depends on whether the halfword at +24 (the subsystem vendor) equals 0x1002, and for a few ids on the halfword at +26 (0x152, 0x10a, 0x172 or 2). The identifiers used have top bytes 0x85, 0x87, 0x88, 0x91, 0x92, 0x95 to 0x99. For id 0x4964 the identifier is 0x85000004 (class 1, flags 7), after rewriting a subsystem vendor of 0x0e11 to 0x1002; for id 0x4e54 it is 0x8820ffff (class 4, flags 0xf). Those two are the identifiers `_r6x_CheckWSBoard` tests.
  Last, both exit paths call a debug stub twice (at 0x12098 and 0x120bc) with the identifier and the flags, then return.
  Which identifier goes with which of the other ids was not followed through the shared exit blocks: unknown.
